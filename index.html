Bleached

As atmospheric CO₂ has risen, how have the frequency and severity of coral bleaching changed, which reef regions were hit hardest, and what else is changing in the water?

What bleaching is

Reef-building corals live with algae inside their tissue that feed them and give them color. When the water stays too hot for too long, corals expel those algae and turn white. A bleached coral is not dead yet, but if the stress lasts, it starves. NOAA calls climate-driven ocean warming the leading cause. Polluted runoff, too much sunlight in shallow water, extreme low tides and even sudden cold can also trigger it.

It matters because reefs are crowded: over a quarter of all marine life relies on them.

After 2016: two global events in eight years

This dataset ends in 2016, but the trend did not. NOAA Coral Reef Watch counts four global bleaching events: 1998, 2010, 2014–2017 and the one confirmed in April 2024. During the third, 68.2% of the world's reef area saw bleaching-level heat. Between January 2023 and September 2025, the fourth reached about 84.4% of reef area in at least 83 countries and territories, the largest on record.

The Global Coral Reef Monitoring Network's 2025 report links the biggest drops in coral cover to 1998, 2010, 2016 and 2023–24, and warns that with a global event every 5–6 years, reefs are expected to keep declining.

How this was made

  1. Collect. Hughes et al. (2018) bleaching records via Our World in Data; NOAA Mauna Loa CO₂; Met Office HadSST 4.2; Hawaii Ocean Time-series pH.
  2. Preprocess. Renamed and typed columns, checked that the four regions add up to the World total (8 of 37 years differ by one event, so World is used for global totals), averaged pH cruises into calendar years and dropped years with fewer than 6 samples, dropped the incomplete 2026 temperature year.
  3. Analyze. Per-decade rates, severe share, Pearson and Spearman correlations, a linear fit of severe events on temperature, and a year-over-year (detrended) check.
  4. Visualize. D3.js: linked line charts, a map, stacked bars, a dumbbell chart and a scatter plot, all driven by one year slider.
